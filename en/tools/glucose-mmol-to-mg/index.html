Convert blood glucose between mmol/L and mg/dL

Some labs and glucose meters report in mmol/L, others in mg/dL. Enter your number and pick a direction to see it in the other unit.

--

This tool is for general awareness and estimation only. It does not diagnose or replace a doctor. Results are estimates and your doctor interprets them for your situation.

How does this tool work?

The rule: 1 mmol/L of glucose is about 18 mg/dL. Multiply mmol/L by 18 to get mg/dL, and divide mg/dL by 18 to get mmol/L.

Example: 7 mmol/L is about 126 mg/dL, and 100 mg/dL is about 5.6 mmol/L.

American Diabetes Association cut-offs: fasting glucose under 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) is normal, 100 to 125 mg/dL (5.6 to 6.9 mmol/L) is prediabetes, and 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or more on two separate tests indicates diabetes. Under 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) is low. Your doctor makes the diagnosis, not a calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is 5.6 mmol/L in mg/dL?

About 100 mg/dL, the upper limit of a normal fasting glucose according to the American Diabetes Association.

How do I convert glucose from mmol to mg by hand?

Multiply the mmol/L value by 18. For example, 6 mmol/L x 18 is about 108 mg/dL.

Why does my meter use a different unit from the lab?

Meters are set to the unit of the country they were sold in. The value is the same; only the way it is written differs.
