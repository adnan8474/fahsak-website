Convert triglycerides between mmol/L and mg/dL

Triglycerides have their own conversion factor, different from cholesterol. Enter your number and pick a direction.

--

This tool is for general awareness and estimation only. It does not diagnose or replace a doctor. Results are estimates and your doctor interprets them for your situation.

How does this tool work?

1 mmol/L of triglycerides is about 88.57 mg/dL. Multiply mmol/L by 88.57 to get mg/dL, and divide for the reverse.

Example: 1.7 mmol/L is about 150 mg/dL.

NCEP ATP III cut-offs: under 150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L) is normal, 150 to 199 borderline, 200 to 499 high, and 500 (5.6 mmol/L) or more very high. It is usually measured fasting, so follow your lab's instructions.

Frequently asked questions

What is 2 mmol/L of triglycerides in mg/dL?

About 177 mg/dL.

Why is the triglyceride factor different from cholesterol?

The molecules have different weights, so a millimole holds a different number of milligrams.
